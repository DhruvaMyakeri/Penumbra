PENUMBRA
POLICY METHOD FINDINGS SUITES GITHUB
PERCEPTION FAULT INJECTION · PHYSICAL AI

Change what the robot sees. Measure how its plan moves.

PENUMBRA takes a real robot recording, repaints what the cameras see with a generative video model, and replays it to a vision-language-action policy. Physics, geometry and motion stay real. Only appearance changes. Then it measures, with a statistical test against a matched control, whether the policy's intended actions shift.

02 · HOW A SHIFT IS MEASURED

Crowds of runs, a matched control, and a shuffle test

A single before-and-after comparison proves nothing when the policy disagrees with itself. So every situation is judged as a group of rollouts against a group of control rollouts, with the video model's own fingerprint cancelled out.

STEP A

Render a no-op control

The episode is passed through the same video model with a prompt asking for no change. Every situation is compared against this, not the raw recording, so re-rendering artefacts land on both sides and cancel.

STEP B

Gate every render

Before the policy sees a frame, a validity gate checks optical-flow drift, structure and temporal motion. A render that moved the scene instead of repainting it is rejected, and an independent vision model reports what each render actually shows.

STEP C

Test the groups

Control and situation rollouts are pooled and their labels shuffled 5,000 times. If random labellings almost never separate as far as the real ones, the shift is real. Two statistics are tested: the arm trajectory and the grasp decision.
